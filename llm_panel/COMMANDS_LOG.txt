PROTOCOL IN ONE PARAGRAPH
Four LLMs (Claude, ChatGPT, DeepSeek, Gemini) act as independent, blind reviewers of the 55-threat catalogue (v1).
Prompt A gives only the architecture and vocabulary and asks for the threats the model expects (completeness test).
Prompts B1-B4 add the threat cards of one layer (L1 16, L2 12, L3 12, L4 15 threats) and ask for STRIDE-tau category,
goals, adversary tier, evidence level and PA-DREAD scores per threat, as one JSON object. One fresh chat per prompt,
memory/history off, no web search, no tools, no attachments, whole prompt pasted as a single message, reply saved unedited.
The catalogue's own classifications and scores were never shown to the models in Round 1.

OPENING OF EVERY PROMPT (identical wording in all prompts; the rest of each file is system description,
27-asset register, trust boundaries, rubric, JSON schema and, for B, the threat cards):
----------------------------------------------------------------------
You are an independent senior security reviewer with expertise spanning satellite systems, air-traffic surveillance (ADS-B, multilateration), GNSS and precise timing, and structured threat modelling (STRIDE, PASTA, DREAD). You are one member of a panel of independent reviewers; you will not see the others' answers.

PURPOSE AND SCOPE
This is a defensive security review for academic research. The material is a threat model of a civil air-traffic surveillance system, written for peer review in a security journal. The goal is to find errors, gaps and misjudged risks in the threat model so that the system's designers can prioritise defences. You are asked to classify and rate threats at the level of abstraction used in standards and academic papers (STRIDE categories, DREAD-style ratings, public technique names). You are NOT asked for, and must not give, attack procedures, parameters, signal specifications, code, tooling or step-by-step methods; no operational detail is needed for this task. Everything you need is in this prompt.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

PAPER FIGURES, COMPLETENESS MAPPING AND PAPER TEXT (added 30 Oct 2026)
  figures/ holds the four figures of the paper section "Independent Review by a Multi-Vendor Panel of Language Models" (Fig_panel_protocol, _agreement, _disagreement,
  _completeness, as PDF and PNG in out/) and the scripts that draw them from results/ (run each fig*.py from inside figures/: python fig_agree.py; python fig2.py; python fig3.py; python fig4.py; python fig1.py).
  fig_agree.py recomputes the 95% percentile bootstrap intervals (1000 resamples, seed 7) used in Fig. 2a and writes out/fig2_alpha_bootstrap.csv.
  Fig. 2c uses the ChatGPT Layer-3 test-retest (responses/chatgpt__B3__run1.json against retest/responses/chatgpt__B3__run2.json): 69 scores, 80% identical, 97% within one point.
  completeness/elicitation_mapping_manual.csv maps the 143 threats the four models listed in Prompt A to the catalogue (C = matches a catalogue threat, P = related: narrower or different, N = no counterpart;
  114 C, 18 P, 11 N; four rows carry a second mapping). completeness/named.json lists, for every catalogue threat, the models that named it. completeness/build_elicitation_mapping.py holds the manual dictionary and writes both files.
  LIMIT: the mapping is a judgement by ONE analyst and is single-coded; a second coder should repeat it before it is relied on.
  Numbers quoted in the paper from this mapping: 46 of 55 catalogue threats named by at least one model (41 with a clear match, 5 only related), 39 by two or more, 23 by three or more, 12 by all four,
  9 by none (T1.06, T1.07, T1.12, T2.09, T2.10, T3.10, T3.11, T4.03, T4.05); per model 37 / 34 / 24 / 25 of 55 (Claude, ChatGPT, DeepSeek, Gemini); threats listed per model 45 / 42 / 26 / 30.
  The 11 listings without counterpart fall in seven themes: integrity-monitor false acceptance, false rejection and alert flooding (3 models); common-mode or insufficient fault independence across the two checks (2); natural clock faults mimicking an attack (1);
  ATM misreading of integrity annotations (1); social engineering of operators (1); orbit-determination feed spoofing (1); satellite geometry degradation (1).
  Other numbers in the paper section (all from results/): alpha and W from agreement_alpha_vs_W.csv; without Claude differs by at most 0.056 (Di weak); leave-one-out alpha of the total score 0.645 to 0.772 over both passes;
  model_vs_catalogue.csv for Fig. 2b (Claude is closest to the catalogue on 4 of 13 measures, ties included, and furthest on one); 708 scored fields = 588 agree + 41 panel-vs-catalogue + 79 split (panel_summary.csv tags;
  'panel vs catalogue' means at least 2/3 of the models, i.e. 3 of 4, agree with each other but not with the catalogue; for scores, an interquartile range of at most 1);
  seven contested threats by the rule in tools/agreement.py: T1.15, T2.03, T1.01, T1.02, T2.10, T1.03, T1.04. Validity verdicts: 212 valid, 3 not_a_threat, 3 duplicate, 2 out_of_scope of 220.
  The catalogue was not changed in response to the panel (the version reviewed is the version reported).
